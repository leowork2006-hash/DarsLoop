# Religious sources and demo scope

Checked 6 October 2026. The public example is an original fictional introductory lesson, read by a local synthetic voice. Its prepared notes and questions are drawn only from its own script. It is not a teacher recording, a fresh ASR/AI run, an approved religious lesson, or a qualified scholarly review.

## References used to check basic definitions

- [HadeethEnc: Islam is built on five, record 66512](https://hadeethenc.com/en/browse/hadith/66512): the publisher lists the five pillars, treats the two testimonies as one pillar, explains five daily prayers, zakah, fasting and pilgrimage. The publisher attributes its narration to Bukhari and Muslim. This is the publisher's attribution; DarsLoop performs no hadith grading. No sacred quotation or publisher translation is reproduced in the demo script.
- [Egypt's Dar Al-Ifta: The 5 Pillars of Islam](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/article/details/65/the-5-pillars-of-islam): corroborates the basic descriptions, distinction of zakah from voluntary charity, dawn/sunset fasting, and ability qualification for pilgrimage.

The original script makes brief descriptive paraphrases of basic definitions. Source pages are linked for checking, not presented as an endorsement or a blanket redistribution licence. Conditions, exceptions, disagreements and personal rulings are intentionally outside this short introduction. Consult a qualified teacher and appropriate authoritative source for them.

## Product trust boundary

A timestamp/page citation demonstrates correspondence to captured material, not that the captured material is religiously correct. Source lookup records remain separate from class answers. Missing, uncertain or instruction-like passages are withheld from study generation; other transcription or model errors may remain. Ruling cues, personal application and hadith grading are routed to a teacher using deterministic guards plus source-only model policy. These are bounded controls, not complete protection against every paraphrase.
